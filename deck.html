Systematic liquidity research

A systematic liquidity-research operation

The deep-tuned book — a per-instrument record, a published cross-section of a 130+ instrument universe.
Abstract

LIQUIDEX is a systematic research operation built on a single principle: institutional liquidity leaves the same structural footprint wherever it trades. Working from that principle, each instrument in a 130+ instrument universe — equities, digital assets and FX — is read down to the institutional behaviour that dominates its order flow and tuned to its own per-instrument recipe through a disciplined optimisation protocol. Eighty cells are complete as of this thesis — 50 equities, 27 crypto and 3 FX majors — of which sixty-five clear the edge bar (profit factor ≥ 1 at ≥ 30 trades). The trustworthy tier is the thirteen symmetric both-direction cells that survive a regime flip; the high-profit-factor majority are long-only fades read through a secular-bull window, so they carry a walk-forward-mandatory caveat. Section 5 turns from the book to its cause — the cross-asset institutional actors structurally positioned to produce the same footprint across equities, crypto and FX, profiled as evidenced hypotheses. All figures herein are in-sample and net of commission and slippage; their out-of-sample validation is the subject of Section 6.

LIQUIDEX Framework — Thesis§1 · The mechanism
Section 1

The mechanism

Institutional order flow is not noise; it is the residue of decisions. A liquidation cascade, a manufactured stop-run, iceberg absorption defending a level, the exhaustion of a one-sided move — each is a behavioural signature, and each repeats.

The engine identifies the signature, attributes it to the institutional behaviour producing it, and resolves that read into a single rule-based position: entry, a structural stop, a target at the opposing wall, a breakeven and a time-stop. It does not merely detect an anomaly; it profiles the actor behind it — what they are doing, where they are going, and whether they are masking it.

An edge that lives in one market is a curve-fit. An edge that recurs across many is a discovery.

The claim under test is narrow and falsifiable. If the edge were specific to one instrument, it would be an artefact of that instrument's history — an overfit. If instead the same behavioural reads recover an edge across many unrelated instruments, the engine is measuring a structural property of institutional markets rather than the idiosyncrasy of a single chart.

The sections that follow set out, in order: the protocol used to tune each symbol (Section 2); the deep-tuned book across the universe (Section 3); two worked examples of the per-symbol read, with the behavioural reasoning behind each (Section 4); the cross-asset actors behind the lever — who produces the same footprint across worlds, and why (Section 5); and the validation work that separates these in-sample results from a track record (Section 6).

LIQUIDEX Framework — Thesis§2 · The protocol
Section 2

The per-symbol protocol

Breadth is meaningless without method. Every symbol is taken through the same protocol. The discipline is deliberately conservative: a behavioural read is admitted only when it improves the risk-adjusted result at its tuned setting, and discarded the moment it does not.

  1. Baseline

    The engine is reduced to its core — every optional read disabled, default parameters throughout. Raw profit factor, drawdown and trade count are recorded as the benchmark to beat.

  2. Greedy forward-selection

    Behavioural reads — cascade-stop, opposing-wall target, the directional-risk gate, risk-velocity confirmation, iceberg absorption, liquidity-sweep exhaustion — are added one at a time, retained only if they improve the risk-adjusted result, and reverted otherwise.

  3. Coupled parameter tuning

    Each retained read is judged at its tuned setting, not its default: its sensitivity is swept in both directions, and a read that cannot pay at any setting is removed rather than left at a default that flatters it.

  4. Two-pass refinement

    The surviving configuration is walked a second time, each secondary parameter nudged and immediately reverted if the equity curve degrades. The accepted recipe is the one no further change improves.

  5. Risk-adjusted scoring

    Every decision is taken on S = PF / (1 + MaxDD), never on profit factor alone, subject to a hard floor of thirty trades. A high profit factor on a small sample does not qualify.

  6. Out-of-sample gate

    A recipe this specific must clear a held-out window before it is treated as real. In-sample strength is the entry condition for that test, not a substitute for it.

We do not search for the setting that makes a chart look good. We search for the read that survives being doubted.

What the book shows

Read blind, the book sorts itself into two structural temperaments. 67 of the 80 are faders — defensives, macro and the broad index complex, where the edge is reverting an exhausted move — and 13 are continuation names, where it rides the break. 54 run long-only, the signature of a passive complex accumulating through a rising market; the 26 both-direction cells, which add a short engine, are the regime-robust minority. That long-only majority is the principal caveat: an edge measured through a secular bull is exactly what an out-of-sample window must test.

Section 4

Worked examples: the per-symbol read

Two symbols from the book, opened all the way up. Together they make the same point from opposite sides: the read is not a setting but an understanding of the actor's temperament, expressed as a configuration.

PEP — when the desk is patient

A liquidation cascade never comes for a consumer staple. PepsiCo is not where leverage blows up; it is where patient, mandate-driven capital accumulates and defends. Its flow is quiet, two-sided and mean-reverting — and that is precisely why a default read fails it, listening for violence in a market that trades on patience.

Reading the desk meant matching the algorithm to the psychology: both directions enabled, the reward-to-risk gate raised so only the cleanest reversals qualify, iceberg absorption set deliberately slow to wait for genuinely defended size, and the risk gate tightened on the short side. The coin-flip became a clean, low-drawdown edge.

TLT — when the move never reaches the wall

TLT is long-duration Treasuries — a macro vehicle, not a tape that liquidity desks push to clean technical walls. Its price is dragged by rate expectations and central-bank language, and the full move to the opposing wall rarely completes, because a data print interrupts it first. Everything one adds to it is fragile: tighten the stop and the chop removes you; widen the target and the move dies before it arrives.

What rescued it was profit-taking discipline — banking 60% of the position two-thirds of the way to the wall — and a wide sweep stop. A buffer-30 stop lifts the result past the earlier 1.232; the whole edge balances on those two levers.

Section 5

The actor behind the lever

The thesis of this document — one behavioural read, many instruments — raises a harder question: why should the same footprint appear at once in a single-name equity, a crypto perpetual and an FX pair that share no fundamental link?

The naïve reading is coordination. The defensible reading is structural coupling: a small, identifiable population of balance sheets, risk engines and trading models now spans all three worlds, so a single risk decision mechanically expresses itself as the same directional flow in each.

At the level that matters — the risk engine, the collateral pool, the trend signal, the inventory book — these are no longer three markets. They are one correlated object with three tickers.

This is an evidenced hypothesis, not an attribution: nothing below claims a named firm executed any specific trade — dealer inventories and intraday positions are not public. Each actor is framed as structurally positioned to produce the footprint, evidenced by public fact, and the coupling is regime-dependent — sharpest in risk-off and vol-spike regimes [LSEG]. Four documented forces drive it.

Force 1 · Common holders

One correlated basket, de-risked at once

The cleanest case is the yen-carry "risk-on basket." The BIS attributes the August-2024 turbulence to the unwinding of leveraged equity and currency trades — ~¥40tn of carry, amplified by margin calls, with equities and FX selling together [BIS 90]. The yen funded AI equities, crypto and EM alike, so one decision to cut the carry sold every leg. The same structure recurs in multi-strategy pod shops, where hundreds of pods share one risk budget and one cross-margined book — a concentration GSAM ties to "synchronised deleveraging during market stress" [GSAM] — and, most mechanically, in risk-parity, whose vol target deleverages every asset the moment cross-asset volatility rises.

Force 2 · Shared market makers

One inventory engine, every venue

The same handful of electronic principals now quote the same risk-on instruments across equities, options, FX and crypto from one engine — so when firm risk limits bind, the bid widens or withdraws across every world at once. XTX forecasts 50,000+ instruments across every asset class from a single engine [XTX]; Jane Street is a named Authorized Participant for spot-Bitcoin ETFs as well as a top equity and ETF maker [Blockworks]; Citadel Securities announced a crypto desk in 2025 [CoinDesk]. The documented failure mode: Wintermute halted quotes across ~60 venues in the October-2025 crash when its hedges broke [Decrypt].

Why one footprint, four forces

Force 3 · Leverage & collateral

One margin call cascades a whole book

This force supplies the directional force and the deadline. A price move pushes a levered book past a threshold — a VaR limit, an exchange maintenance margin, a repo haircut re-set — triggering price-insensitive, deadline-driven selling that trips the next threshold. The documented anchor is the Treasury cash-futures basis trade: the OFR records $2.5tn of hedge-fund repo financing of basis and curve trades and warns stress forces funds to "deleverage by unwinding positions" [OFR], run as high as ~50:1 [Fortune]. Its crypto analogue is the liquidation cascade — open interest and one-sided funding build clusters that act as fragility points [Glassnode]; the ~$19bn October-2025 unwind showed market makers withdraw in lockstep once auto-deleveraging broke their hedges [Decrypt]. The trigger is a number, not a view — which is why "fade the capitulation" is an edge, and why mistaking forced flow for informed repricing is where it loses money.

Force 4 · Factor crowding

The identical signal, traded everywhere

This explains the most striking part of the footprint: the symmetric, both-direction trend cells that appear together in crypto, FX and equity indices. The object is time-series momentum [Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen] — one vol-scaled signal, long winners and short losers, run across hundreds of futures. AQR, Man AHL, Winton, Transtrend and Aspect all run it live and are measured on the same SG Trend benchmark [SG]; Man AHL's own research names crypto among its markets [Man]. Because the signal is direction-agnostic, one trigger level fires long-or-short orders across markets on the same day — and the sell side documents both legs, modelling ~$33bn of CTA equity selling on a downside break and ~$30bn of shorts whose covering drives the rallies [Goldman]. The both-direction symmetry is the crowd, not any one name.

The four forces stack. Crowding sets where stops and liquidation levels cluster — the same technical points across all worlds. Leverage supplies the price-insensitive force and the deadline once price arrives. Common-holder de-risking sells the whole basket on one decision. Shared market makers pull the bid simultaneously, turning the move into a vacuum. The crowded longs and trend-followers are the fuel; the liquidity engineers and forced sellers are the flow that consumes it — which is why one read works on a staple, a coin and a currency, and why the thirteen symmetric both-direction cells, surviving a regime flip, are the trustworthy tier of the book.

Three worked profiles

Each is selected for evidentiary strength, and each is an evidenced hypothesis: a public mandate that positions the actor to produce the footprint, never a claim that it did so on any given candle.

Jane Street — the basket-arbitrage market maker

Holds >10% of North-American equity trading and the leading ETF market-maker share, ran ~$700bn/month of ETF turnover, and is a named Authorized Participant for spot-Bitcoin ETFs [Blockworks · GlobalTrading]. Cross-asset concern: the ETF create/redeem mandate makes the book structurally long-and-short ETF-versus-basket pairs across equity, bond and crypto at once, so the same absorb-and-hedge logic recurs in every world. Caveat: the AP active on a given day is not public, and the disputed SEBI interim order [Business Standard · CNBC] is kept only as the documented shape of the archetype — not a finding of manipulation.

ExodusPoint — the self-disclosed levered-basis book

The rare case confirmed by the holder: its own investor letter disclosed that the Treasury basis trade lifted quarter-end performance, with basis and rates over a fifth of risk allocation and explicitly named FX, commodity and government-bond legs [Investing.com]; it sits among the funds running that trade toward ~50:1 [Fortune]. Cross-asset concern: one global-macro risk engine holds Treasury basis, FX carry and equity-index futures as a single correlated complex, so a repo or haircut shock forcing the unwind propagates to the other legs under one VaR. Caveat: the letter confirms the book, not any specific forced sale.

The systematic-trend cohort — the symmetric cells

AQR — the source of the time-series-momentum result and a live operator — with Man AHL, Winton, Transtrend and Aspect, all measured on the same SG Trend benchmark and several sharing AHL lineage [SG · AQR · Man]. Cross-asset concern: one medium-term trend factor replicated across ~10 of the largest funds, with overlapping prime brokers and the same modelled trigger levels, fires correlated long-or-short orders across equity indices and FX on the same day — the direct generator of the both-direction cells. Caveat (load-bearing): the crypto leg is thinnest — SOL specifically is risk-on beta spillover, not documented trend positioning, and Transtrend has publicly declined bitcoin futures [Transtrend]. The defensible attribution is to the crowd, not any single fund.

The argument, and its limits

The claim is deliberately narrow. A documented population of cross-asset balance sheets, risk engines, market-maker inventories and trend signals is structurally positioned to produce the recurring footprint, evidenced by public fact. It is not trade-level attribution — that is unprovable from public data, and is not claimed.

Where the evidence is strong, and where it is thin. The strongest direct evidence is the ExodusPoint self-disclosed multi-leg book and the Goldman/BofA both-direction CTA models — public, holder- or desk-confirmed. The weakest, explicitly flagged, is the SOL-specific crypto leg, which is risk-on beta spillover rather than confirmed positioning. The standing caveat is regime-dependence: the cross-asset correlation that powers the unified footprint is sharp in risk-off and vol-spike regimes and can decouple in calm or idiosyncratic episodes. The honest summary is that the evidence justifies the hypothesis and does not close it.

Section 7

The system, and access

The research is delivered as three instruments, invite-only on TradingView. One engine decides the trade; two companions make its ranking and its risk legible.

LIQUIDEXThe engine

5,000+ lines of Pine v6. Reads liquidation cascades, order flow, order blocks, iceberg walls and liquidity sweeps, and resolves them into one position — entry, structural stop, and a target at the opposing wall.

KeeperLive ranking

Runs the engine across the whole book and ranks every name live by profit factor, momentum and risk — so the operator sees the leader the engine prefers, not a guess.

ProphetRisk gate
