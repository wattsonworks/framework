01
The mechanism

There is a mechanism here that identifies a liquidity behaviour which recurs across different assets.

"An edge that lives in one market is a curve-fit. An edge that recurs across many is a discovery."

Institutional order flow is not noise — it is the residue of decisions. Resting liquidity, manufactured sweeps, iceberg absorption, the exhaustion of a cascade. Each is a behavioural signature, and each repeats. The engine reads it and resolves it into one rule-based position — entry, structural stop, target, breakeven, time-stop.

And it does not merely detect the anomaly — it profiles the actor behind it: what they are doing, where they are going, and whether they are masking it. The configuration that profits on a chart is the fingerprint of who controls its flow. Read across a sector, those fingerprints resolve into a handful of repeating institutional desks.

That is the layer a desk, a partner or an allocator is looking for: not a signal that fires, but a read on the institution behind the tape — repeatable, explainable, and present wherever institutions trade. The rest of this page is the evidence, the method, and one tuning in full.

02
The protocol

How we read a desk — one instrument at a time

Breadth means nothing without method. Every instrument runs the same disciplined protocol: isolate the engine, add only what pays, tune only what survives, discard anything that needs a curve to look good. This is the exact process behind every row in the table below.

01

Baseline

Strip the engine to its core — gates off, defaults on. Record the raw profit factor, drawdown and trade count. This is the coin-flip we have to beat.

02

Greedy forward-selection

Add one behavioural read at a time — cascade-stop, wall-target, the risk gate, risk-velocity, iceberg absorption, sweep exhaustion. Keep a read only if it improves the risk-adjusted result; revert it the moment it doesn't.

03

Coupled tuning

A read is judged at its tuned setting, not its default. Each kept read has its sensitivity swept up and down; if it can't pay at any setting, it is dropped — not parked at a default that flatters it.

04

Two-pass refinement

Walk the surviving configuration a second time, nudging each secondary setting, reverting the instant the curve degrades. The final recipe is the one nothing further improves.

05

Risk-adjusted scoring

Every decision is made on S = PF ÷ (1 + drawdown), never profit factor alone, with a hard floor of 30 trades. A spectacular curve on twelve trades does not qualify.

06

Walk-forward gate

A recipe this specific must clear out-of-sample before it is real. Every deep-tuned cell carries that flag. In-sample strength is the entry ticket, not the verdict.

"We don't search for the setting that makes a chart look good. We search for the read that survives being doubted."

04
Anatomy of a tune · behaviour × algorithm

Two temperaments. Two tunes.

Two of the three names that failed the greedy pass were not dead — they were misread. Opened all the way up, each teaches the same lesson from the opposite side: read the actor's temperament, then make the algorithm behave like it.

PEP — when the desk is patient

A liquidation cascade never comes for a soda company. PepsiCo is not where leverage blows up — it is where patient, mandate-driven capital accumulates and defends. Its flow is quiet, two-sided, mean-reverting: the temperament of a holder, not a gambler.

That temperament is exactly why the default read failed it. At baseline PEP is a coin-flip — profit factor 0.749. The standard greedy pass barely moved it, to 0.849 — one of only three names in fifty that would not clear. The engine was listening for violence in a market that trades on patience.

Reading the desk meant matching the algorithm to the psychology. Both directions enabled — a staple gets faded at its highs and bought at its lows. The reward-to-risk gate raised, so only the cleanest reversals qualify. Iceberg absorption set deliberately slow — five hits, not three — to ignore noise and wait for genuinely defended size. Sweep-exhaustion tuned to a high-volume threshold, acting only when a level is truly spent. The risk gate tightened on the short side, because a staple punishes shorts that fight the accumulation. Then a second pass — nudging each knob, reverting on every degrade.

Eleven toggles, six tuned parameters, a revert at every step the curve worsened — and the coin-flip became a clean, low-drawdown edge. Reproduced live, not asserted. The point is not the number. It is that the number required reading a market's temperament and tuning a machine to match it: behavioural judgement and algorithmic discipline, each covering what the other cannot see.

The honest caveat. A recipe this deep is also where overfitting hides. PEP carries the walk-forward flag like every deep-tuned cell — in-sample proof is the entry ticket, not the verdict.

TLT — when the move never reaches the wall

TLT is long-duration US Treasuries — a macro vehicle, not a tape that liquidity desks push to clean technical walls. Its price is dragged around by rate expectations and central-bank language; the full move to the opposing wall rarely completes, because a data print or a policy line interrupts it first.

So the greedy pass left it for dead — 0.809, a loser. And everything you add to it is fragile: tighten the stop and the chop knocks you out; widen the target and the move dies before it arrives. The edge sits on a knife's edge.

What rescued it was not another signal — it was profit-taking discipline. Bank 60% of the position two-thirds of the way to the wall, and let a small runner chase the rest. You stop demanding a clean completion the macro will not give, and take what it offers — with a wide sweep stop to survive the chop and a both-sided cascade-fade behind it. The dead name becomes a real, if fragile, edge: PF 1.272 at 17.66% drawdown across 205 trades, +18.05% — a wide buffer-30 sweep stop lifting it past the earlier 1.232.

PEP's lesson, in reverse. PEP needed patience; TLT needs to not be greedy — and the whole result balances on two Partial-TP numbers. It wears its fragility honestly: a 1.272 at 17.66% DD is a thin edge that, more than any cell on this page, has to clear walk-forward before it is anything at all.

Cross-instrument invariance
Every instrument resolves to one of two structural temperaments — and the split is consistent.

Read blind, the book sorts itself. The large majority of the deep-tuned names are faders — defensives, macro and the broad index complex, where the edge is reverting an exhausted move — with a continuation minority that rides the break. Most run long-only, the fingerprint of a passive complex accumulating through a secular-bull window — which is why almost every equity cell carries a walk-forward-mandatory flag. The exception proves the rule: in crypto, SOL sorts into a both-direction trend-rider, monetising the downside its long-only cousins cannot. An edge fitted to a chart dies on the next chart; an edge that sorts a whole universe into the same temperaments is a property of the market — pending out-of-sample confirmation.

The actor behind the lever

Why one footprint repeats across unrelated worlds

The same structural footprint appears at once in an equity, a crypto perpetual and an FX pair that share no fundamental link. The defensible reading is not coordination but structural coupling — a small population of balance sheets, risk engines and trend models now spans all three worlds, so one risk decision prints the same flow in each. An evidenced hypothesis, not an attribution.

01

Common holders

One correlated basket — the yen-carry risk-on trade, the multi-strategy pod book, risk-parity — de-risked on a single decision.BIS · GSAM

02

Shared market makers

The same electronic principals quote equities, FX and crypto from one inventory engine; when risk limits bind, the bid withdraws everywhere at once.XTX · Jane Street · Wintermute

03

Leverage & collateral

A VaR limit, a margin call, a repo haircut — one number forces price-insensitive selling that cascades a whole cross-margined book.OFR · Glassnode

04

Factor crowding

One time-series-momentum signal, run by ~10 of the largest trend funds, fires long-or-short across markets the same day — the source of the symmetric both-direction cells.AQR · Man · SG Trend

Jane Streetbasket-arbitrage market maker

A named spot-Bitcoin-ETF Authorized Participant and top equity/ETF maker — structurally long-and-short ETF-versus-basket pairs across equities, bonds and crypto at once.

ExodusPointself-disclosed levered basis

Its own investor letter names FX, commodity and government-bond legs of a Treasury-basis book run toward ~50:1 — one VaR over a correlated macro complex.

The trend cohortthe symmetric cells

AQR, Man AHL, Winton, Transtrend, Aspect — one trend factor, overlapping prime brokers, the same trigger levels. The crowd, not any single name.

Strongest evidence: the ExodusPoint self-disclosed book and the Goldman/BofA both-direction CTA models. Weakest, explicitly flagged: the SOL-specific crypto leg, which is risk-on beta spillover.
